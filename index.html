I started
with the surface.

My strongest fashion work begins before the final garment. I look at what a fabric can hold, what a botanical mark can become, how a line changes when it is stitched, and how a small detail can give a piece its identity.

This portfolio puts the making first.
Technology comes later, when it gives the work another way to be understood.
01 / material + handwork

Surface,
stitch & trace.

Two things keep returning in my work: what nature leaves on cloth, and what the hand leaves behind. Eco-printing gives me marks I cannot completely control. Hand embroidery lets me build a mark slowly, stitch by stitch. I wanted both processes to be seen as design work, not decoration added at the end.

Eco-printing / botanical transfer / from arrangement to garment

The print is the beginning,
not the finish.

The arrangement of leaves and petals becomes part of the textile before the garment exists. I like that the final surface carries small differences in colour, pressure and shape instead of behaving like a perfectly repeatable print.

03 / development

Idea,
pattern, piece.

I do not want the finished garment to be the only evidence of design. The boards, measurements, pattern work and product details show how an idea moves toward something manufacturable and wearable.

making decisions

Fabric is measured, silhouettes are adjusted, details are placed, and the garment is tested against the person who will wear it. I am interested in the point where an aesthetic idea has to become a real product.

04 / Tangy Stitch

A brand I can
keep building.

Tangy Stitch is where my fashion work becomes larger than an individual garment. I have worked across product, material language, packaging, storytelling and the digital experience, trying to make the brand feel rooted without making it feel stuck in the past.

BRAND SYSTEM / SELECTED DEVELOPMENTidentity · packaging · garment storytelling · signature thread
What I want the brand to hold

Clothes first.
Then the story.

I want Tangy Stitch to grow as a family-run Indian label that treats clothing as something made, worn and lived with. The details matter because they give the product a reason to be remembered.
